# David's Opinions

A map of durable beliefs, principles, and taste.
Respect these when a task would benefit from David's perspective.

## AI agents, orchestration, and developer tools

- Agents are first-class collaborators. Design the workflow and tooling around them.
- Agents amplify good engineers but do not replace human judgment and taste.
- The work shifts to specs, verification, and review. Producing code is no longer the hard part.
- Stay deliberately skeptical. Agents can create as much review burden as they save, so use them with intent.

## AI labs, markets, and openness

- Stay vendor-neutral. Avoid lock-in to any single lab and keep the stack portable.

## Software engineering, craft, and process

- The real bottleneck is clear thinking and problem definition. Once the problem is framed well, the rest follows.
- Human accountability stays explicit. Never let agents add themselves as commit co-authors; record AI-assistance metadata instead.
- Favor the actor model and OTP-style supervision for concurrency and fault tolerance.
  Isolate failures so they are recoverable rather than catastrophic.
- Invest most in end-to-end and integration tests on real user flows.
  Unit-test the tricky or risky logic; do not chase coverage for its own sake.
- Push strong typing everywhere (Rust, TypeScript strict, typed contracts) so errors surface at compile time, not runtime.

## Product, startups, and organizations

- Taste and polish are real competitive advantages, not nice-to-haves.
- Distribution beats product more often than builders admit.
- Small, focused teams outperform large ones.
- Polish before shipping. Do not release something until it is genuinely good.
- Hold a performance budget. Latency is a feature.
- Optimize for user simplicity. Fewer features done well beat feature breadth that adds cognitive load.

## Career, learning, and work

- Mastery and depth beat shallow breadth.
- Learn by shipping real things, not by consuming courses.
- Optimize for leverage and ownership over titles and status.
- Async, deep-focus work beats meeting-heavy culture.

## Platforms, discourse, and trust

- Own your audience and domain. Do not build on rented land.
- Default to building in public and open source.
- Be wary of engagement-driven algorithmic feeds and their incentives.
- Trust is earned through consistency and transparency over time.

## Society and institutions

- Technology is net-positive for society on balance.
- Institutions are slow; bottom-up change matters more.
